    • Spectral display: hepatic venous flow is laminar, so the spectrum is a narrow band with a clear window beneath it. The brightest band is the modal velocity (the velocity of most red cells); the outer edge is the peak velocity.
    • Normal pattern: S deeper than D. Doppler velocity tracks the descents: the x descent drives S and the y descent drives D.
    • Rising RA pressure with a stiff, full RA blunts the x descent first, so S shrinks until S < D, while a prominent y descent keeps D large.
    • With systolic reversal, the a wave and reversed S fuse into one large retrograde wave above the baseline, followed by an isolated antegrade D wave.
    • Tricuspid regurgitation fills the RA during systole: the x descent is replaced by a large c-v wave, and S blunts, then reverses (above baseline) when severe.
    • Atrial fibrillation removes the a wave on both tracings and blunts S, so S < D can occur without congestion.
    • VExUS hepatic vein grading: normal S > D; mildly abnormal S < D; severely abnormal S reversal. It is applied only when the IVC is greater than 2 cm and is integrated with portal and intrarenal venous Doppler.
    • Other confounders: cirrhosis can flatten the waveform; positive pressure ventilation and pericardial disease alter the pattern. Always correlate with the ECG.
